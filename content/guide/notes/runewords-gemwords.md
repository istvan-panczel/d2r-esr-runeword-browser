The vanilla rule still holds: put the right socketables, in the right order, into a white item of an allowed type with the right number of sockets. ESR has three families:

- **ESR runewords** use ES runes, and many have low level requirements, for example [Luck](rw:Luck) or [Strength](rw:Strength).
- **LoD runewords** use LoD runes and keep their vanilla names, such as [Insight](rw:Insight) or [Enigma](rw:Enigma), but with ESR stats. Many names, Insight among them, exist in both families with different runes and bases, so check which runes the card shows.
- :term[Gemword]s use gems instead of runes, so the gems you pick up anyway become gear. [ArchDimeron](gw:ArchDimeron) is one example.

A card shows the runes, bases and stats, for example the early LoD runeword Stealth:

::card[rw:Stealth]

The [Runewords](page:/runewords) and [Gemwords](page:/gemwords) pages here list every recipe with its runes, allowed bases, socket count and level. Some runewords also accept charms as a base, which matters for the Veteran's Odd Charm ([[noobs-charm]]).

### Practical tips

- Get a base with exactly the right sockets ([[socketing]]). The [Best Base](bestbase:Enigma) tool compares bases for a runeword.
- Trying a runeword is free: a Key in the cube returns the base and every rune.
- Since 3.3 the Archaeologist specialization perk Power Overwhelming also lets you make runewords in magic items.
- You cannot forge a runeword, but you can D-stone it. D-stoning done before you make the runeword survives removing the runes; D-stoning done after is lost when you remove them ([[dstoning-and-mapling]]).
- Early on, farm the Countess: she has her own rune drop, ES runes and Decals, on every difficulty. Cube up the low runes you find as well ([[rune-families]]).
